# Merchant Visual Direction Brief

## Brief and sources
Collect audience, product facts, intended emotion, channel, brand constraints, budget and production limitations. Use owned or licensed reference assets; browsing is optional when references are supplied. A text moodboard and image-slot list is the fallback.

## Direction development
Translate abstract adjectives into visible choices: composition, lighting, material texture, palette, typography, density and motion if relevant. Separate inspiration from assets licensed for final use. Group references by the role they demonstrate rather than collecting a visually attractive pile.

Prepare distinct directions only where they represent different decisions. For each, explain why it fits the product/audience and what production requirements it creates. Include an anti-reference explaining what to avoid without treating a personal taste rule as universal design law. Cultural associations are hypotheses needing audience review, not stereotypes.

Choose a coherent direction using the brief and constraints. Specify hero/support/detail asset roles, crop ranges, type hierarchy and factual-product boundaries. Avoid copying a competitor’s distinctive identity, layout or protected assets. A moodboard is not evidence of likely conversion uplift.

## Deliver
Return annotated reference board/list, chosen direction, concrete production brief, rights status and unresolved constraints. Commissioning, purchasing stock or generating/uploading assets requires appropriate scope.
