## Specific changes

A product positioning and message-house matrix linking buyer alternatives, attributes, benefits, proof and objections.

- Name what a buyer would do without the product, including delay, keep an existing item or choose another category. Separate observed alternatives from the team’s guesses.
- Map each distinctive attribute to a practical benefit and a buyer-valued outcome. Remove chains that stop at an internal feature name.
- Use customer language to identify push, pull, anxiety and habit. Preserve supporting quote IDs and avoid treating a few interviews as market prevalence.
- Reconcile differentiators to what can ship now, by SKU and market. Aspirational future features and unsupported superiority claims cannot anchor today’s promise.
- Build one core promise with a small set of pillars; each pillar needs an audience, supporting fact, proof source and objection response. Expose proof gaps rather than filling them with superlatives.
- Test the promise against named alternatives. If the same statement honestly fits each alternative, narrow the situation or articulate a supported distinction. Publish neither exclusive nor comparative claims merely because a positioning exercise prefers them.
- State brand purpose separately from evidence of customer preference or willingness to pay. Proposed differentiation must survive comparison with named alternatives; brand equity cannot be inferred from internal enthusiasm or a uniqueness sentence.

Removed upstream mandatory registries, benchmark gates, mutable connector paths and software-only assumptions. Replaced them with merchant-supplied artifacts and explicitly labeled decisions. Kept task-specific method; replaced fixed industry targets with declared merchant constraints. Synthetic cases below are authored for this adaptation, not claimed customer outcomes.
