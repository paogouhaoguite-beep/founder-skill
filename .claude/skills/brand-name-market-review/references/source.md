## Specific changes

A market-by-market brand-name screening matrix with linguistic fit, domain observations and unresolved clearance questions.

- Assess meaning, pronunciation, spelling, recall and category fit separately for each target market. Preserve uncertainty about unfamiliar slang or dialects.
- Distinguish generic/descriptive/suggestive characteristics as screening observations, not a legal registrability verdict.
- Test spoken-to-written confusion and diacritics/transliteration with realistic customer scenarios. Avoid treating English sound associations as universal.
- Check dated domain and public name-use evidence through appropriate sources. Domain availability does not establish trademark clearance, and a marketplace listing does not establish seller authority.
- Compare candidates using merchant priorities and explicit tradeoffs; do not rank by an invented universal weighted score or extension prestige.
- Return a shortlist, linguistic review gaps, dated domain observations and separate professional clearance questions before spending on registration or launch.
- Set merchant-specific criteria before generating candidates; explore descriptive, coined, evocative and other relevant directions before narrowing. Use pairwise tradeoffs and spoken/recall checks. Do not impose token-efficiency, AEO-first, alphabet-only or fixed-letter rules, and never label a screen as legally defensible clearance.

Removed upstream mandatory registries, benchmark gates, mutable connector paths and software-only assumptions. Replaced them with merchant-supplied artifacts and explicitly labeled decisions. Kept task-specific method; replaced fixed industry targets with declared merchant constraints. Synthetic cases below are authored for this adaptation, not claimed customer outcomes.
