# Editorial quality and SEO/GEO acceptance

## A useful article
- Match the keyword's actual reader need and language; answer promptly without a long generic introduction. Develop enough detail to resolve the question, without padding to a universal length.
- Add merchant-specific knowledge from verified specs, care instructions, product differences or supplied experience. Distinguish a manufacturer's claim, independent evidence and editorial judgment. No invented "we tested", research, testimonials or credentials.
- Use descriptive H2/H3 structure; comparisons and steps when they help. Define ambiguous entities/materials. Support meaningful non-obvious claims with direct, preferably primary, sources near the claim. Do not copy competitor paragraphs or mechanically paraphrase their article.
- Give qualified recommendations, contraindications/limitations when relevant and one natural next action. Avoid turning every paragraph into a sales pitch. Do not expose internal cost accounting, tool names or research instructions in the article body.

## Search fields and technical limits
Use the primary concept naturally in the title/main heading and relevant opening copy, with related terms where meaningful. Titles and descriptions are accurate and distinct; there is no mandatory keyword density, length or guaranteed snippet display. Verify actual publishing-field limits before writes. Supply an excerpt and proposed readable handle; do not change an existing URL just to insert a keyword. A future canonical URL is a proposal until the final route is known.

Do not add a second H1 to a theme that already renders the title. Article markup must remain readable HTML with crawlable links and real image sources. Check the current theme before adding structured data: do not duplicate its BlogPosting/Article entity. Only supply supported author, publisher, image and actual dates; unknown required facts make a schema proposal non-deployable. FAQ sections can be useful but do not imply merchant FAQ rich-result eligibility or require FAQ schema.

## Acceptance before delivery
1. Reader can answer the main question and take an appropriate next step.
2. All requested articles exist in full; repeated intents are consolidated or flagged rather than padded into near-duplicates.
3. Verifiable claims have sources; hypothetical examples are explicitly hypothetical. No false experience or current-price/availability promises.
4. Product names, material, care and image identity match the store evidence.
5. Image and link checks in assets-links-delivery.md pass, or the concrete gap is stated outside the article.
6. SEO fields and body are complete; no unresolved placeholders in publish-ready output.
7. The verdict is ready for review, needs asset/link verification, or saved draft with readback. Never issue a fabricated SEO/GEO score or guarantee ranking, indexing, conversion or AI citation.
