# Merchant Team Capacity Plan

Use for staffing a campaign, catalog release or support workload; production-machine sequencing belongs to operations planning. Inputs: named roles and skills, contracted hours by dated week, leave, meetings, BAU/service commitments, project estimates/ranges, deadlines/dependencies, outsourcing/hiring lead times and budget. Spreadsheet or pasted tables work without calendar/project connectors; read authorized exports only.

Build a role-by-week ledger. Available project hours = contracted hours minus leave, nonoverlapping meetings, BAU commitments and explicitly chosen contingency reserve. Prevent double subtraction where meetings already sit in BAU. Do not impose universal utilization targets or treat every person's hour as interchangeable.

Map each task to required skill, estimate range, prerequisites and earliest start. Allocate only qualified capacity after dependencies are satisfied. Sum demand within each role/week and show gaps; unused designer hours cannot cover an oversubscribed editor unless the owner confirms capability. Distinguish utilization of gross paid time from allocation against net project capacity.

Stress-test estimate ranges, absence and delayed assets. Compare keeping scope, deferring tasks, reducing scope or using qualified help. Hiring has a start date, ramp time and cost; it cannot fill an earlier gap. Show work displaced and deadline consequences rather than promising that overtime is available.

Return role/week capacity ledger, task allocation, bottleneck, alternatives with cost/time assumptions and one recommended decision for the owner. Unknown estimates remain ranges or unallocated, never zero. Record the date for revisiting actual versus estimated effort. Do not book calendars, hire, contact contractors or change employee schedules automatically.
