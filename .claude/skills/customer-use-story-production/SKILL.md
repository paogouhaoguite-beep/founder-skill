# Customer Use Story Production

Use when a merchant needs a full customer story, beyond a short testimonial. Collect product/version, target reader, customer access or existing permissioned notes, factual timeline, alternatives, exact quotes, outcome records and publication/identity permissions. Local transcripts and supplied records are sufficient; no interview, outreach or publication is automatic.

Interview before drafting when evidence is missing: ask about the last concrete situation, what was tried, purchase hesitation, actual use and what changed. Ask open questions about measurements rather than suggesting a favorable numeric range. Preserve timestamps and quote wording; separate customer interpretation from independently documented facts.

Create a claim ledger with baseline, follow-up, unit, population, window, source and competing explanations. A personal improvement is not a typical result or proof of causality. Calculate changes only on comparable observations; disclose self-report and measurement differences. Do not force a number or two quotes when none are available. A qualitative use story is valid when labeled honestly.

Write a customer-led arc: context, difficulty and alternatives, product use and limitations, observed outcome, relevant next step. Keep the product's contribution proportional to evidence. Use a measured result in the headline only if verified and permissioned; avoid implying every buyer will obtain it. Mark proposed quotations as drafts, never verbatim evidence.

Deliver the complete article with quote/claim ledger and unresolved approvals. Offer anonymization where needed without fabricating composite identities. Check that exact wording, identity, photos and channels fall within granted rights. Only publish or contact the customer when separately authorized; otherwise return a reviewable draft.
