## Specific changes

A category-structure and macro-risk evidence table, capability/option comparison, scenario arithmetic and a bounded expansion recommendation.

- Define the category boundary and merchant decision. Separate category attractiveness from whether this merchant can win; market growth or funding announcements alone do not establish profitable demand.
- Build a five-forces evidence table: entry barriers, supplier concentration/switching constraints, shopper alternatives/price sensitivity, substitutes meeting the same job, and rivalry/differentiation. Qualitative high/medium/low labels require an explicit rationale and contrary evidence; do not average them into a scientific score.
- Screen political/economic/social/technical/legal/environmental changes only where a causal path to demand, costs, access or risk is stated. Distinguish enacted requirements from proposals and forecasts from measured history; verify current authoritative rules before acting.
- Separate internal strengths/weaknesses from external opportunities/threats. Match actual capabilities to opportunities and vulnerabilities to threats. Avoid using generic category growth as a merchant strength.
- Model decision-sensitive base/downside scenarios using supplied units, prices and costs. If computing historical or forecast CAGR, use (end/start)^(1/years)−1 with positive comparable endpoints; label projected endpoints. A change in definition invalidates the comparison.
- Deliver an evidence-linked market memo with option, economic mechanism, uncertainty, reversible test and review trigger. Select frameworks that answer the question; no required report length, force count score or unsupported quantified upside.

Removed upstream mandatory registries, benchmark gates, mutable connector paths and software-only assumptions. Replaced them with merchant-supplied artifacts and explicitly labeled decisions.
