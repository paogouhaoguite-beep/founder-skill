# Evidence Based Shopper Archetypes

## Inputs
Require a decision to support, participant-level evidence, collection dates and recruitment description. Optional analytics or survey exports can corroborate behavior. A local evidence matrix suffices. If there is no research, deliver labeled assumption profiles and a research plan, not discovered personas.

## Build decision-relevant distinctions
Extract observed or reported goals, constraints, workarounds and purchase situations by participant. Distinguish inferred motivation from actual behavior. Cluster by differences that would change a design or merchandising decision, not imagined ages, hobbies or stock-photo identities.

For each cluster, record source IDs and quotations, sample coverage, contrary cases, situational overlap and an explicit design implication. A person can occupy different contexts over time; do not force a permanent type when evidence describes a temporary need. Merge clusters that imply the same decision, unless their constraints require meaningfully different treatment.

Compare archetypes along the relevant dimensions, such as fit certainty versus delivery urgency, and explain which the current decision prioritizes. Record who is absent from the research. Do not infer trait prevalence from qualitative recruitment or prescribe a minimum interview count as proof of validity.

## Deliver
Return an evidence matrix and concise archetype cards with behavior label, context, goals, constraints, observed versus self-reported evidence, assumptions and decision consequence. Date the set and set a revalidation trigger when products, markets or recruitment change. Do not use inferred sensitive traits for targeting; these are research aids, not personal customer records.
