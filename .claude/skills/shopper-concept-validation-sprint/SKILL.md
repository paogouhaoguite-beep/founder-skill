# Shopper Concept Validation Sprint

## Inputs and fallback
Collect decision, high-risk assumptions, evidence so far, concept alternatives, participants, time budget and implementation constraints. A written plan/storyboard is enough without prototyping tools. Do not promise that a fixed five-day schedule or five participants validates a business.

## Sprint protocol
Map the shopper’s current situation and the decision to be made. Select one consequential uncertainty that can be tested within the sprint; distinguish desirability, comprehension/usability, feasibility and economics.

Sketch multiple solutions before choosing. Compare them against the evidence and constraints, record the decision rationale and build only the parts necessary to expose the assumption. A realistic prototype should preserve product facts and distinguish unavailable features; do not collect real payments for an undisclosed fake product.

Prepare neutral tasks and a consistent interview guide. Define observable success/failure signals and what result would change the decision before sessions. Pilot prototype paths and task wording. Recruit for relevant behavior and document who is missing.

During sessions record actions, quotes, prompts and failures separately. Synthesize recurring and contradictory evidence; identify what the prototype cannot answer. Decide revise/test again/stop/proceed to a larger test, without treating qualitative enthusiasm as sales demand.

## Deliver
Return sprint question, alternative matrix, prototype/storyboard, test script, observation ledger and decision memo with remaining risks. Building, recruiting and publishing a prototype require appropriate scope.
