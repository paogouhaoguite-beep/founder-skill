## Specific changes

A source-backed brand voice guide with naming glossary, contextual tone matrix and before/after copy examples.

- Collect the merchant’s own shipped material and separate website, customer-support, campaign and founder voice. Do not use competitors as evidence of this brand’s voice.
- Extract diction, sentence rhythm, person, formality and tone changes from specific passages. Mark new-brand choices as proposed, not historically observed.
- Build a naming table for products, variants and collections, including capitalization, discontinued synonyms and distinctions such as waterproof versus water-resistant.
- Define stable voice traits with do/don’t examples, then a tone matrix for product education, delay apologies, promotions and community replies. A playful product voice need not be playful during a complaint.
- Rewrite representative original passages without changing specifications, offer conditions or substantiated claims. Preserve factual uncertainty even if stronger wording sounds more persuasive.
- Deliver dated rules and a change log. If an existing approved guide contradicts the inferred corpus, surface the conflict for the merchant instead of silently replacing it.
- Validate the guide on a withheld owned sample: annotate observed agreement and counterexamples, refine context-specific rules and preserve exceptions. Source word/piece minimums are not proof of representativeness; disclose thin or uneven corpora instead of generating numeric certainty.
- Audit surface vocabulary/punctuation, structural syntax/rhythm and semantic stance separately. Return deviation, source rule, reader consequence and a proposed rewrite while preserving facts and approved claims. Do not force a 200-word lexicon or VAS85 pass score; require observable examples and a context-specific review.
