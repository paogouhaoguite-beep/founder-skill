# Product Motion Generation Brief

## Inputs
Collect rights-cleared product references from relevant angles, immutable product attributes, desired action/camera motion, setting, aspect ratio and generation budget/tool scope. A storyboard/reference manifest is sufficient without an image/video generator. Do not invent hidden product geometry from a single view.

## Generation plan
Separate identity references from style references. Build a compact continuity sheet covering SKU/color, geometry, labels, proportions, material and fixed scene relationships. Name what may change and what must remain locked. For multi-shot work, define anchor frames and state transitions before generating motion.

Use a small storyboard/contact sheet to inspect composition and sequence. A2×2 board is one planning artifact, not four unrelated deliverables. Check anchor frames first, then generate short bounded motions where tool support and scope permit. Avoid asking the model to change product shape while moving the camera.

Review every candidate for identity drift, incorrect labels, impossible mechanisms, continuity, extra objects and unintended claims. Log accepted/rejected assets and reference lineage. If a material feature is wrong, stop or revise the shot; do not choose the “least bad” candidate and call it product-accurate.

## Deliver
Return reference bible, shot prompts/specifications, anchor images if generated, continuity issue log and actual approved outputs/status. Generated scenes are illustrations, not evidence of product tests. Paid generation, uploads and publication remain scoped actions.
