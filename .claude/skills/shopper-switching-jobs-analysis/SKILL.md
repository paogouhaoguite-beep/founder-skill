# Shopper Switching Jobs Analysis

## Evidence and scope
Use dated interviews, diaries, reviews or support notes with source IDs, plus the merchant decision. Work locally in a table; research tools are optional. Paraphrases can support tentative interpretation but cannot become verbatim quotes. This analysis does not prove why the entire market buys.

## Analyze actual episodes
Locate a concrete situation, trigger, old solution or workaround, alternatives considered, choice and subsequent use. Separate purchase choice from the later decision to use the product: a purchase alone does not establish a recurring use habit. Include doing nothing, borrowing and alternatives outside the product category where the evidence supports them.

Name a job as desired progress, independent of a particular feature: “When [situation], I want [progress], so I can [outcome].” Reject a rewritten feature request that would disappear if the named product disappeared.

For each job, build a four-force table: push away from the old situation, pull toward the new option, anxiety about change, and habit supporting the current solution. Attach evidence to each populated cell; mark missing cells unknown. These are explanatory categories, not numeric forces to add into a predictive equation.

Separate functional, emotional and social dimensions only where supported. Do not infer status or insecurity from a purchase. Compare cases and contrary cases, state participant coverage and recruitment limits, and group related subjobs without erasing different circumstances. Evidence strength depends on directness and context, not a three-person threshold.

## Deliver
Return a switching timeline, job inventory, force/evidence matrix, purchase-versus-use gaps and next research questions. Translate one supported friction into a testable product or information change. Do not turn inferred jobs into customer targeting labels or automatically launch campaigns.
