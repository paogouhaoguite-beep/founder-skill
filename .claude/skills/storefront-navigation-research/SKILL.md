# Storefront Navigation Research

## Inputs
Collect catalog/category inventory, shopper goals and vocabulary, current navigation, target segments and constraints. A spreadsheet and text tree are sufficient; research platforms are optional. Do not confuse internal product taxonomy with shopper mental models.

## Research design
Inventory labels and content scope, resolving duplicate/ambiguous categories. Use open card sorting to discover grouping language or closed sorting to test an existing structure; choose based on the question. Cards need comparable granularity and understandable content, not leading category clues.

Define recruiting rationale and record exclusions. Analyze participant groupings and labels with source IDs, preserving disagreements. Do not turn a tiny convenience sort into population percentages or force one universal hierarchy.

Test a candidate text tree with realistic goal tasks that avoid naming the destination. Define correct acceptable destinations and direct versus indirect success before sessions. Record first choice, path, backtracking, completion and misunderstood labels. Separate tree-findability issues from visual interface issues.

Revise labels/structure from evidence, compare alternatives and retest high-impact paths. Cross-list products where it serves distinct shopper routes and the store supports it, without creating misleading duplicate categories.

## Deliver
Return proposed tree, label glossary, card/task protocol, evidence/path table and remaining uncertainty. Changing live navigation or recruiting participants requires scope.
