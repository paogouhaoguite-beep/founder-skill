# Domain Expert Interview Planning

## Inputs and fallback
Obtain the product/category decision, unresolved questions, relevant geography/date, known evidence, expert role and recent hands-on experience, session length and conflicts of interest. Work from supplied biographies and a document editor when no research tools exist; label credentials unverified. Do not book or contact people without permission.

## Interview preparation
Define what the expert can know from direct work versus opinion. Screen with “Describe the last time you personally handled this process.” Build a perspective map: practitioner, buyer, vendor and independent specialist as relevant. A prestigious title alone is not evidence; record vendor or advisory incentives.

Create a funnel from recent experience to broad context, then decision-specific episodes, then deeper probes. Each core question names its learning objective. Ask for an example before asking why it mattered. Ladder upward toward constraints and priorities, and downward toward observable details; stop when further probing repeats speculation or crosses confidentiality boundaries. There is no mandatory number of “why” repetitions.

Keep the merchant hypothesis out of question stems. Probe counterexamples with the same care as supporting examples. Ask for public documents or nonconfidential aggregates where appropriate, not competitor secrets or customer-identifiable records. Finish by asking what was missed and which public evidence could test the account.

## Artifact and interpretation
Return eligibility criteria, perspective gaps, timed guide, neutral probe bank and evidence log with firsthand/recalled/opinion status. Separate expert assertions from independently verified facts. A single expert does not estimate market prevalence. Any quote intended for publication needs separate permission and exact wording review.
