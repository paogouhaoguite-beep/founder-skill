# Shopper Usability Testing

## Setup
Collect the decision, prototype or storefront URLs, target shoppers and accessibility needs, device context, tested version, tasks allowed and recording permissions. Prefer a staging store and test payment method. A document-only plan is the fallback when no browser or participants are available; do not claim a test ran.

## Task protocol
1. Turn the research questions into realistic goal scenarios without naming interface controls. Define an observable end state, start state, relevant product facts and failure conditions before testing.
2. Recruit for the behavior/context under study rather than convenience alone. Explain exclusions and gaps. Choose sample size from learning needs and diversity, not a promise that five people find all issues.
3. Choose moderated sessions for diagnosis or an unmoderated protocol only when instructions and prototype paths are sufficiently clear. Pilot the tasks. Prevent accidental live purchases and exposure of real account data.
4. Establish consent and ask participants to think aloud without teaching the interface. Log task start/end, result, errors, moderator prompts and evidence timestamps. Record abandoned or blocked tasks; do not drop them from the denominator.
5. Separate unassisted success, assisted completion, incorrect completion and noncompletion. Define valid attempts and censoring before calculating task success. Time-on-task comparisons need the same timing rules and should state treatment of failed tasks.
6. Group issues by mechanism and impact, preserving device and participant context. Task friction observed in a session is not a measured conversion uplift. Connect each fix to evidence and a retest condition.

## Deliver
Provide screener, consent and task script, success definitions, observation sheet, issue register and retest plan. If using a standardized questionnaire, preserve its official wording and scoring rather than inventing a shortened score. Publishing recordings, contacting participants or making store edits remain separately scoped actions.
