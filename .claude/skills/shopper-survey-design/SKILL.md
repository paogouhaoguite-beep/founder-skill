# Shopper Survey Design

## Define the decision
Collect the population, decision, constructs, recruitment channel, expected response process, languages, existing tracking questions, sensitivity and analysis needs. Use documents and a spreadsheet locally; survey platforms are optional. A draft is not permission to send or collect personal information.

Decide whether the question is descriptive, exploratory or causal. A survey of volunteers describes respondents; it cannot by itself establish that a store change caused a result. For formal subgroup or experimental comparisons, specify effect size, precision and design before choosing sample size; do not apply a universal 100-response rule.

## Construct the instrument
Map each construct to a question and intended analysis. Ask recent behavior with a concrete reference window before attitudes. Screen on relevant experience, then route respondents so nonusers are not forced to rate an experience they never had. Place sensitive optional items last.

Use one concept per question. Remove leading adjectives, assumed satisfaction, moral pressure and double-barreled wording. Use balanced labeled scales, exhaustive nonoverlapping answer options and not-applicable/prefer-not-to-answer choices when meaningful. Frequency options must use actual intervals. Preserve historical wording for time comparisons or explicitly mark a series break.

Write the full branching logic and response coding, including multiple selections and missingness. Pilot comprehension and navigation with people resembling the audience; record observed completion time instead of asserting a questions-per-minute constant. Review mobile and assistive-technology behavior. Revise, version and freeze before fielding.

## Deliver and analyze safely
Return construct map, final ordered questionnaire, branch table, recruitment plan, cognitive pilot criteria and analysis specification. Define each reported denominator: contacted, eligible, started, answered item or completed. Show item nonresponse separately. Optional verbatims generate hypotheses and must not be quoted as population frequencies. Do not silently weight a convenience sample into representativeness.
