## Specific changes

A brand-extension decision memo with evidence-backed adjacency, architecture options and staged validation.

- List the brand associations and advantages supported by actual customer or operating evidence. Separate durable capabilities from one successful ad or unsupported loyalty claims.
- Map the proposed extension to a real buyer occasion and identify why trust in the core product would transfer; distinguish adjacency from mere logo reuse.
- Check manufacturing/service credibility, quality level, price tier and distribution conflicts. A profitable extension can still contradict the core promise.
- Compare master-brand, endorsed sub-brand or separate-brand options using clarity, operating cost and customer confusion, not an invented moat score.
- Define a small evidence-gathering pilot with success and harm signals, including core-customer reaction and operational capability.
- Return a go/test/hold decision with assumptions, required proof and reversible investment stages. No brand extension is declared defensible simply because it is first-party data or a community.

Removed upstream mandatory registries, benchmark gates, mutable connector paths and software-only assumptions. Replaced them with merchant-supplied artifacts and explicitly labeled decisions. Kept task-specific method; replaced fixed industry targets with declared merchant constraints. Synthetic cases below are authored for this adaptation, not claimed customer outcomes.
