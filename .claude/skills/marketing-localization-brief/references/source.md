## Specific changes

A market-specific marketing localization brief separating immutable product facts from adaptable language, imagery and offer presentation.

- Distinguish language translation from market adaptation. Map which buying situations, proof, payment expectations and delivery promises actually change.
- Build a locale brief with audience evidence, tone, terminology, units, currency presentation and support availability. Never assume language equals country.
- Audit source offers for eligibility, deadlines, stock and service promises before adapting them; a phrase cannot make an unavailable benefit available.
- Create a transcreation matrix: preserve exact facts, adapt examples and imagery, and flag regulated or ambiguous claims for appropriate review.
- Specify native review and in-context layout checks for product, checkout and campaign surfaces. Include text expansion and right-to-left needs when relevant without prescribing an unverified framework migration.
- Return approved versus pending decisions with owners. Measure locale performance using comparable traffic and fulfillment conditions, not translation word count.
- Classify each content block as translate, localize or transcreate, and keep approved regulatory wording exact. Build local keyword evidence rather than translate US keywords; log cultural hypotheses as hypotheses, not stereotypes. Request an in-market reviewer for meaning, terminology and cultural context; back-translation can reveal drift but is not certification. Replace weighted quality scores with issue-level review and current jurisdiction-specific evidence.

Removed upstream mandatory registries, benchmark gates, mutable connector paths and software-only assumptions. Replaced them with merchant-supplied artifacts and explicitly labeled decisions. Kept task-specific method; replaced fixed industry targets with declared merchant constraints. Synthetic cases below are authored for this adaptation, not claimed customer outcomes.
