# Worked example

All names, inputs and results below are synthetic.

Synthetic supplied documents (fictional study labels, not publications): L1 lab test: fabric X withstands20 washes under protocolA; L2 customer-use study: finished bagY,30participants,8weeks, no wash testing; L3 abstract reports fabricX strength but gives no wash protocol. Merchant asks “Can we say our bag lasts20washes?” Product uses fabricX but seam/dye/construction differ from L1 specimen.
Evidence table: L1 supports specimen/protocol durability, finished-bag applicability unresolved; L2 supports only observed use in that sample/window; L3 insufficient methods. Decision: no finished-bag20-wash claim established. Next evidence: matched finished-product testing with wash conditions. Three documents are not three independent replications.

## Acceptance scenarios

1. Only an abstract is available. Label missing methods and do not infer sample details or adverse-event absence.

2. A meta-analysis and its included trial are both supplied. Preserve both records but avoid counting the trial twice as independent evidence.
