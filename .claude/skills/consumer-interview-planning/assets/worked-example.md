# Synthetic worked example

Input: A bag merchant wants to learn whether interior organization influences purchases; 25-minute remote sessions with recent buyers and non-buyers. Recording has not been authorized.

Finished guide:
- 0–3 min: “We are learning how people choose day bags. There are no right answers. You may skip a question or stop. May I take anonymous notes? We will not record this session.”
- 3–7 min, context: “Tell me about the last occasion you needed a day bag.” Probe: “What did you carry?”
- 7–15 min, must-cover choice: “Walk me through how you chose one.” Probes: “What other options did you consider?” and “What information did you look for?”
- 15–21 min, actual use: “Tell me about the first time you used it.” Probe: “What happened when you needed something from it?”
- 21–25 min: “What have I missed?” Note uncertainties and end without showing a concept.

Removed question: “How frustrating was the lack of pockets?” It assumes both the problem and emotion. No conclusion about pocket demand is available yet.

Boundary scenario 1: Only loyal repeat buyers volunteer. Label recruitment bias; recruit abandoned or first-time purchasers separately before comparing their choices.
Boundary scenario 2: A participant declines notes or recording. Continue only within the permission they give, or end the session; do not reconstruct verbatim quotes from memory.
