# Consumer Interview Planning

## Brief and tools
Collect the merchant decision, purchase/use context, recruitment source, participant eligibility, session length, hypotheses, recording permissions and incentive terms. Keep merchant hypotheses on a separate researcher sheet. A text editor is sufficient; a call or recording connector is optional. Without participants, deliver an unfielded guide, never interview findings.

## Build the guide
1. Map each decision uncertainty to an observable past episode and one learning objective. Prioritize must-cover objectives within the actual time available; do not impose a universal question or participant count.
2. Start with a plain introduction: who is conducting the study, voluntary participation, skip/stop options, use of notes and separate recording permission. Confirm eligibility with factual closed questions when needed.
3. Warm up on the last relevant purchase. Ask an open question before introducing product concepts: “Walk me through the last time you chose a bag for a day out.” Follow the participant's sequence from trigger through alternatives, purchase and use.
4. Add neutral probes: “What happened next?”, “What did you compare?”, “What was driving that?”, “Was that typical?” Separate observed actions, recalled actions and hypothetical preferences in the note-taking sheet.
5. Place concept reactions after the unprimed account. Do not interpret willingness-to-buy statements as purchases. For repeat waves, version the guide and mark comparable questions.
6. Read aloud and remove compound questions, praise, suggested answers and assumed pain. Assign soft time budgets and a drop-first list; leave space for unanticipated but relevant episodes.

## Deliver
Return a recruitment screener, consent script, timed objective/question/probe table, must-cover flags, note columns (participant ID, timestamp, verbatim/paraphrase, observation/inference), and completed question-bias review. Explain which population the recruitment misses. Recruiting, messaging, recording and publishing quotes require their respective authorization; drafting a guide authorizes none of these.
