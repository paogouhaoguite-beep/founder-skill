# Worked example

All names, inputs and results below are synthetic.

Synthetic annual inputs: net sales $200,000; COGS $120,000; operating profit $20,000; average inventory $30,000; average receivables $5,000; credit sales $100,000; average payables $12,000; credit purchases $120,000; 365 days. Gross margin=40%; operating margin=10%; inventory days=91.25; receivable days=18.25; payable days=36.5; cash conversion cycle=73 days. These are diagnostic values, not pass/fail benchmarks.

A separate synthetic one-year valuation assumes FCF1=$10,000, next-year FCF=$10,200, discount rate10%, perpetual growth2%, excess cash$5,000 and debt$20,000. Terminal value=10,200/(.10−.02)=$127,500; enterprise value=(10,000+127,500)/1.10=$125,000; equity=$110,000. Discounted terminal value$115,909 is 92.7% of EV: estimate is terminal-assumption dominated. At12% discount with same growth/FCFs, EV=(10,000+102,000)/1.12=$100,000; equity=$85,000. No rate is represented as a current market estimate.

## Acceptance scenarios

1. Current liabilities are zero: current/quick ratios are undefined, not zero or an infinite quality score. Report underlying balances.

2. Terminal growth is at least the discount rate, or credit purchases are absent. Reject the respective terminal calculation or payable-days claim until assumptions/data are corrected.
