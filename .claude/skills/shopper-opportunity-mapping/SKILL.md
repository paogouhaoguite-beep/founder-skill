# Shopper Opportunity Mapping

## Inputs and working medium
Collect a bounded merchant outcome with measurement window, research snapshots, current shopper journey, constraints and candidate ideas. A text tree and spreadsheet are sufficient. Without research, label opportunity nodes as hypotheses; do not retrofit customer evidence around a preferred feature.

## Construct the decision tree
Write one outcome at the root, such as fewer fit-related returns among a defined order cohort. Distinguish the outcome from a project deliverable. Map the shopper’s current end-to-end experience, including alternatives outside the store; each pain or need cites a snapshot or observation.

Arrange outcome → opportunities → solutions → tests. Split broad needs into specific moments where a change is possible. Generate competing solutions for an opportunity rather than treating one design as inevitable. Compare opportunities using observed reach, severity, frequency, merchant constraints and uncertainty. Unknown reach stays unknown, not a fabricated market score.

List each solution’s desirability, usability, feasibility and commercial viability assumptions. Identify consequential assumptions with weak evidence. Choose the smallest ethical test that can change the decision, specifying stimulus, participants, observable result, stopping rule and decision criterion in advance. An intent click is not a purchase; disclose unavailable concepts rather than pretending they can be ordered.

After a test, attach result and contrary evidence to the relevant node, then keep, revise or retire the proposal. Choose an update cadence the merchant can sustain; no required weekly interview count or arbitrary maturity score.

## Deliver
Provide the source-linked current-state map, opportunity tree, alternative comparison and test backlog with owners and next review trigger. This is planning; recruiting, prototypes on a live store and data collection need the appropriate scope.
